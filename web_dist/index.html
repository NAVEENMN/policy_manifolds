What am I editing?

Each edge computes a cubic B-spline plus a weighted SiLU residual. Diamonds are the spline coefficients, not points the curve must pass through. The curve includes both terms. Each node sums its incoming edges.

The mint dot shows the edge’s current input and output. A dashed curve is the untouched checkpoint. Edits affect the live actor only, stay in this browser session, and are never written over your checkpoint. Switching environments preserves edits until the page is reloaded.

The original ghost starts from the same state and follows its own policy. Restart to compare a full episode from identical initial conditions. Reset seeds are reproducible within this app.

A HANDS-ON EXPERIMENT

Reshape a decision.

  1. Choose an environment with a saved policy from the dropdown in the top bar, then press Run policy.
  2. Select an edge in the network or one of the curve cards.
  3. Drag a diamond up or down. You are changing a real spline coefficient; the next action uses the new curve.
  4. Watch the simulation, next action, and progress trace. The dashed ghost keeps the original policy.
  5. Use Restart for the same starting state, Undo to reverse an edit, or Restore trained policy to reset everything.

Try a middle control point on an angle or velocity edge. CartPole chooses between two pushes; Pendulum applies continuous torque. Acrobot and MountainCar choose among three actions. Use “Compare 20 episodes” for a broader comparison.

The checkpoint’s actor runs in your browser. Classic Control physics runs in the browser; Box2D and MuJoCo use the local Python server. Larger networks have an input-group selector, and every actuator has its own control indicator. Saved evaluation badges distinguish target-passing, below-target, and incomplete runs. Each environment loads its own saved actor; Pendulum normalizes angular velocity by 8 and maps its action head through tanh to ±2 N·m. InvertedPendulum uses its saved observation normalization and continuous cart controls from −3 to +3. The action head stays fixed. Diamonds edit B-spline coefficients at fixed x positions; these are not Bézier tangent handles.
